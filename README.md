

I built this portfolio risk analyzer to get past just reading about risk and diversification and actually see it work in code. It takes historical stock prices, calculates daily returns and volatility for each one, and then goes a step further by working out covariance between pairs of stocks, because two assets with the same average return can carry very different risk depending on whether they move together or not. That covariance is what turns a simple return calculator into something that actually models portfolio risk, showing how combining stocks that move in opposite directions can lower overall volatility. Along the way I hit real bugs , a stock with just one price point, empty return lists, redundant recalculations , and fixing those taught me as much as the math did. The project is tested with pytest, covers edge cases most tutorials skip, and outputs a clean JSON report per stock. It's a small project, but it's the one that finally made "diversification reduces risk" click for me instead of just being a sentence in a textbook.
